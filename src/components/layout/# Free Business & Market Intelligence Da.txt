# Free Business & Market Intelligence Data Sources — Developer Reference

Every source below is genuinely free — official free tiers, open government/public datasets, or open-source self-hosted tools. No paid-only entries.

One honest note up front: I'm not going to help find ways to bypass paywalls, evade rate limits, or use unofficial/leaked credentials to get paid data for free — that crosses into ToS-violation and unauthorized-access territory regardless of the reason. But as you'll see below, there is a *lot* of real, legitimate free ground to cover — most serious market-intel systems are actually built on a mix of free government/public data plus a handful of paid layers only where free genuinely runs out (mainly: paid social APIs and premium e-commerce anti-bot scraping).

| Source | What you get | Link | Free tier reality (2026) |

## 6. Open-source scraping stack (the free way to fill API gaps)

A lot of the data above (pricing pages, review text on sites without an API, competitor site structure) has no official free API — the standard free approach is to run your own scraper against publicly accessible pages.

| Tool | What it does | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrapy** | Python crawling framework, best for large-scale static-HTML crawls | scrapy.org |
| **Playwright** | Headless browser automation (handles JS-heavy sites) | playwright.dev |
| **Crawlee** (Node.js/Python, from the Apify team) | Batteries-included crawler: queues, retries, session/proxy handling built in | crawlee.dev |
| **BeautifulSoup** | Lightweight HTML parsing (pairs with `requests`) | crummy.com/software/BeautifulSoup |
| **Common Crawl** | A free, petabyte-scale open crawl of the public web, updated monthly — you can run large-scale trend/market analysis without scraping anything yourself | commoncrawl.org |
| **Apify free plan** | $5/month in free platform credit, plus a store of pre-built open scrapers ("Actors") for sites like Amazon, SimilarWeb, LinkedIn, SEC EDGAR, Google Trends | apify.com/store |

**On proxies/CAPTCHAs**: open-source scrapers are free to *run*, but if a target site actively blocks bots you'll hit IP bans/CAPTCHAs — that's the one place where "free" often breaks down in practice, since good residential proxy pools are a paid service. There's no legitimate free workaround for that; if a site is aggressively anti-bot, the honest options are (a) slow down and respect its rate limits, (b) look for an official API instead, or (c) accept that a paid proxy layer is the cost of that particular data source.

### Legal posture on scraping (US, as of 2026 — not legal advice)
The controlling precedents are **hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn** and **Meta v. Bright Data**: courts have consistently held that scraping *publicly accessible* data (no login required, no bypassing of technical access controls) does **not** violate the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. However:
- Breaching a site's Terms of Service by creating fake accounts or scraping behind a login is a **separate, real risk**.
- GDPR/CCPA still apply if you're collecting personal data (names, emails, faces) — public availability doesn't exempt you from privacy law.
- Copyright: scrape *facts* (prices, specs, ratings) freely; don't republish large chunks of *creative/written* content (full review text at scale, articles) without a license.
- Rule of thumb most teams use: if you can see it in a logged-out incognito browser, it's low-risk to scrape; if it requires a login, treat it as off-limits without explicit permission.

## 8. Suggested system architecture (given your stack)

Since you already work in Django/DRF, FastAPI, Celery, Redis, Postgres, and AWS, a natural free-first shape for this system:

1. **Ingestion layer**: Celery beat-scheduled tasks per free source (one task type per API/scraper), writing raw payloads to S3 and a Postgres `raw_events` table.
2. **Normalization layer**: Celery workers parsing raw payloads into a common schema (`entity`, `metric`, `value`, `source`, `timestamp`) — this is what lets you compare, say, EDGAR revenue against Reddit sentiment against Google Trends interest on one timeline.
3. **NLP/enrichment layer**: Run an open-source sentiment model locally (via HuggingFace) instead of paying per-call for sentiment scoring.
4. **Analysis/serving layer**: FastAPI endpoints exposing aggregated trend/comparison data.
5. **Respect each source's rate limits explicitly** in your Celery task config — this is the main way "free" stays free (SEC's 10 req/sec, Reddit's 100 QPM, YouTube's daily quota, etc.).
